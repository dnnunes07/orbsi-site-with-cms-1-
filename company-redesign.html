Orbsi is a company redesign firm for founder-led businesses working through founder dependency, the point where decisions, standards, and context still route through one person even after the company has grown and managers are in place.

Orbsi redesigns company structure, decision rights, roles, and leadership so responsibility has somewhere to live other than the founder.

Founded by Danielle Nunes, a Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist (LMFT 101166, California) and former senior operations director who spent fourteen years inside a large behavioral-health organization, ending with three programs, more than one hundred employees, and over $21 million in public-sector contracts.

But many of the ways your company operates were created for a much smaller business.

Roles evolved as needs came up. Processes were added along the way. Decisions stayed with you because it was faster and easier at the time.

Now, your team is looking to you for answers, the company is slowing down to figure things out, and too much still depends on what you know or personally step in to solve.

Company redesign is the work of deciding how your company needs to operate now, based on the business you have already built and the future you want it to support.

We help you figure out what needs to be clarified, changed, strengthened, or built, including the structure, systems, roles, and leadership your company now needs, so it can run with more consistency.

Before you redesign your company, decide what you are redesigning it for.

Many founders assume the next goal is simply to keep growing.

More customers. More employees. More locations. More revenue.

But growth is not the destination.

The better question is:

What do you want this company to make possible for you?

Because the company you need depends on what you want your future to look like.

Scale

You still want to grow.

You may want more locations, a larger team, new services, or a bigger footprint.

But you do not want every stage of growth to require more of your time, attention, and involvement.

The company needs stronger leaders, clearer responsibilities, and better ways of working so growth does not keep making you more necessary.

Sell

You may want the option to sell one day.

Not necessarily tomorrow, but you want to know you are building something another person could confidently buy and operate.

That means the value of the company cannot live only in your relationships, your judgment, or everything you personally know how to do.

Step Back

You may still care deeply about the company without wanting it to require so much of you.

You may want to spend less time in the day-to-day, focus only on the work you want to keep, or create more room for your family, health, or other parts of your life.

The goal is not to disappear.

It is to be involved because you choose to be, not because the company cannot function without you.

Succession

You may already know who you want to lead the company next.

It could be a family member, a longtime employee, a partner, or someone you have not hired yet.

But choosing the person is only one part of it.

The company, the team, and the future leader all need to be prepared for the transition.

Your destination changes what your company needs.

A company preparing to scale needs something different from one preparing for a sale.

A founder who wants to step back may need a different leadership team and a different role than someone preparing for succession.

That is why we begin with the future you want.

Then we look at the company you have today and decide what needs to change in the structure, systems, roles, and leadership to make that future possible.

Founder dependency

Why the work keeps coming back after you hand it off.

Most founders in this position have already tried the obvious thing. You handed something off. It came back. You told yourself it was faster to handle it, and it was, and now you are back where you started with one more piece of evidence that nobody else can do it.

In the beginning, you were the thing holding it together. You held the standards, the customer knowledge, the priorities, and the judgment. You were, in effect, the operating system the business ran on, and at that size it was the right design. Then the business grew, and the company was never redesigned to hold any of it.

What you are dealing with is founder dependency, and it is a different problem than poor delegation. It shows up for two reasons at the same time.

Reason one

Structural

The task moved, but the responsibility did not. Someone was handed work without the context, the decision authority, or the defined standard that would let them finish it without checking. So it comes back to you, and it should, because the company never actually transferred it. Nothing about that is a people problem.

This is what decision rights are for. Your managers need to know what they can decide on their own, what they should tell you about afterward, and what genuinely needs your approval. Until that exists somewhere other than your head, bringing it to you is the safest thing they can do, and they are right to do it.

Reason two

Behavioral

Answering quickly, reviewing the work, stepping in when something looked off, all of that lowered your uncertainty and all of it worked. Your team learned to wait for you. You learned that stepping in feels better than wondering. Neither of those makes you controlling or a poor delegator.

Advice about delegation only addresses the second reason, and only on the surface. Building systems only addresses the first. When you change one and not the other, the pattern returns within a few weeks, and it starts to feel like proof that this cannot be fixed.

Company redesign changes both. The structure changes so the work has somewhere else to live, and the founder’s role changes so it stays there.

Danielle is a licensed therapist and a former operations director, which is why Orbsi can name what is happening in the structure and what is happening in the founder at the same time, rather than treating one as the explanation for the other.

What gets redesigned?

Every company is different.

Some need stronger leaders. Others need better hiring, clearer roles, stronger performance practices, or a different company structure.

The goal is not to build everything.

It is to identify what your company needs now and redesign the parts that will make the biggest difference. Company redesign often includes work in the following areas.

Company Structure

As companies grow, responsibilities have a way of collecting around the founder.

Roles overlap. People are not always sure who owns what. Important work falls through the cracks because everyone assumes someone else is handling it.

We help redesign how the company is structured so people know what they are responsible for, who makes which decisions, and how work moves from one person or team to another.

Leadership

Growing companies need leaders, not just managers.

That means deciding who should lead, what they should be responsible for, what decisions they should make, and what support they need to become stronger leaders over time.

Sometimes that means developing the leaders already on the team. Sometimes it means hiring new ones. Sometimes it means designing a right-hand role so the founder can step away from work that should no longer depend on them.

Hiring, Onboarding & Offboarding

How someone enters and leaves the company matters.

We help companies create hiring practices that make it easier to identify the right people and onboarding that gets new employees up to speed quickly so they can begin generating or protecting revenue.

We also help put the right offboarding process in place for voluntary and involuntary departures, including the conversations, documentation, access, transfer of responsibilities, and company property that need to be handled.

Performance

Employees should know what is expected of them.

Leaders should know how to coach them.

The company should have clear and consistent ways to recognize strong work, address concerns early, document important conversations, conduct meaningful annual performance reviews, and decide what happens when someone is no longer the right fit.

Compensation & Career Growth

People want to understand how they can grow within a company.

We help companies think through compensation, raises, bonuses, promotions, career paths, and leadership opportunities so employees understand what growth looks like and what is expected of them.

Growth should not feel random.

We help companies align people to the right roles, develop them over time, and promote them when they are ready. We call this Align. Develop. Promote.

Systems & Processes

As companies grow, people naturally create their own ways of getting work done.

Over time, that can lead to inconsistency, confusion, and important work depending on what one person happens to know.

We help document how work gets done, decide where information should live, and create more consistency without making the company feel overly rigid.

AI & Company Knowledge

We do not believe AI replaces people. We believe it helps people work faster and make better use of what the company already knows.

But AI still needs people to train it. It needs your expertise, your standards, your processes, your understanding of your customers, and the way your company communicates and makes decisions. The more it understands your company, the more useful it becomes.

We help companies organize what they know, build AI workspaces for different departments, and teach teams how to work alongside AI while keeping human judgment, leadership, and oversight at the center.

Is Company Redesign Right for You?

This is for you if...

  • Your business has grown, but the company has not kept up.
  • Too much still depends on you.
  • You keep answering the same questions, reviewing the same work, or stepping in to solve problems your team should be able to handle.
  • You have people in leadership roles who need clearer expectations, more confidence, or support becoming stronger leaders.
  • You do not want to lead every part of the company yourself and need help preparing other people to take on more responsibility.
  • You know the company could run better, but you are not sure what needs to change first.
  • You are preparing to scale, sell, step back, or plan for succession.
  • You are willing to invest the time, attention, and follow-through needed to make the changes work.

This is probably not for you if...

  • Your business is still in its earliest stages and does not yet need this level of structure.
  • You are looking for a quick fix.
  • You want templates, SOPs, or documents without changing how the company actually operates.
  • You want to avoid making leadership decisions the company already needs.
  • You are not willing to spend the time helping us understand the business, make decisions, and put the changes in place.
  • You want someone to fix individual problems instead of changing the company that keeps creating them.

Not wanting to lead every part of the company does not make you a poor fit.

In many cases, that is exactly why this work is needed.

You do not have to be the person who leads everything. But you do need to be willing to help identify, prepare, and support the people who will lead alongside you—or in your place.

  • Your team knows who is responsible for what, who makes which decisions, and where to go when something needs attention.
  • Your leaders can answer questions, guide their teams, and keep work moving without waiting for you to step in.
  • New employees get up to speed faster because they have the training, information, and support they need from the beginning.

  • The year starts with a plan the whole company can see, not a direction that lives in your head.
  • You are still connected to the company, but it no longer requires you to be involved in every decision to keep moving forward.

Most importantly, the company is better prepared for the future you want—whether that means scaling, selling, stepping back, or preparing someone else to lead.

Common questions

Questions founders ask before starting.

Why do I keep taking work back after I delegate it?

Because the task moved but the responsibility did not, and because stepping in reduced your own uncertainty in the moment. The person was usually handed work without the context, the decision authority, or the defined standard that would let them finish it without you, so it returns. And each time you take it back, both you and your team learn a little more firmly that it should come to you.

What is founder dependency?

Founder dependency is when a company’s decisions, standards, context, and judgment still route through one person, even after other people have been hired to own the work. The business usually still functions. What it cannot do is operate at the same standard without the founder present, which is different from the business failing and is why it is easy to postpone.

I hired managers, but they still bring every decision to me. What went wrong?

Usually nothing went wrong with the hire. A title does not create decision authority. If nobody has written down what a manager can decide alone, what they should inform you about, and what actually needs your approval, then checking with you first is the reasonable choice. They are protecting themselves from being wrong, and until the line is drawn they cannot know where it is.

What are decision rights?

Decision rights are the written record of what each role can decide without asking, what it decides and then reports, and what it escalates. They usually include real numbers, such as spending limits or discount thresholds, so the boundary is not a matter of interpretation. A manager might approve any purchase under five thousand dollars on their own, report anything between five and twenty thousand, and bring anything above that to you before committing. Most growing companies have never written this down, which is why decisions collect around the founder by default rather than by choice.

Should I hire a fractional COO?

Sometimes. A fractional COO is the right call when the company genuinely needs an experienced operator running a function day to day, and you do not want that job. It is the wrong call when the underlying problem is that the company has no defined roles, decision rights, or standards, because then the operator becomes the new place all the work collects and the dependency has moved rather than resolved. A useful test is to ask what would still be standing a year after that person left. If the honest answer is that everything lived with them, the company needed structure rather than a seat.

Is my business too dependent on me?

The useful test is not whether the business survives while you are gone. It is what happens to the standard. If work keeps moving but quality drifts, or decisions wait for your return, or your team handles what is urgent and postpones anything requiring judgment, then the company is running on your presence rather than on its own structure.

Can I fix this without stepping away from my business?

Yes, and stepping away is not the goal. The goal is that your involvement becomes something you choose rather than something the company requires. Some founders finish this work and stay just as involved, in different work. Others reduce their day-to-day role. What changes either way is that the company holds its standards without you having to hold them.

What is company redesign?

Company redesign is intentionally changing how a company is built so it can support what the business has already become. It covers who makes which decisions, who owns what, how work is handed off, how expectations are set, how leaders lead, how performance is addressed, and what should and should not still depend on the founder.

How long does it take?

Every company starts somewhere different. Rather than beginning with a fixed timeline, Company Redesign begins with understanding where the founder wants the business to go, what role they want to play, and what the company needs to become.

Most engagements continue for several months because redesigning a company and helping it operate differently takes time.

What happens when the engagement ends?

The goal is not for Orbsi to become another person the company depends on. The goal is for the company to become less dependent on the founder.

Some companies transition into Founder Advisory after the redesign. Others continue independently. Every engagement is different.
